Charts that say where the line is

Today every chart in the app answers “how much?” and none of them answers “compared to what?”. The goal is never drawn — it is implied by a fill color one step apart from the neutral fill, on the same hue. This page proposes the missing vocabulary: a reference rule, a target band, a real missing-data mark, a two-level breach scale, a pace marker, and a cutoff track — then rebuilds all four production charts with it.

C2

Goal shapes

The root cause of most of C1 is that the system has one idea of a goal — “bigger fill is better” — and five kinds of goal. Name the shape first; the chart treatment then follows from it instead of being improvised per tracker. Every new tracker declares its shape in the spec before any UI is chosen.

Floorat least

Protein, gym minutes. Reference rule at the floor; bars above it are --a500. Overshoot is fine and must stay visible.

Ceilingat most

Caffeine count, kcal. Same rule, inverted meaning: crossing it is the breach, so the bar takes --warn plus a hatch.

Bandbetween

Sleep, weight, mood. Both directions are a miss. A band, never a line — this is the shape the app currently has no answer for.

Cutoffbefore

Last caffeine, last meal. The axis is the clock, so lateness is a position past a line — legible before any color is read.

Presenceany / none

Gym, CBT record. There is no magnitude, so there must be no bar height. Blocks, not columns — height would invent precision.

Do Declare the shape in the tracker spec

Add a Goal shape line to NEW_TRACKER_SPEC.md alongside metric and cadence. The chart type falls out of it; the reviewer can check one word instead of a screenshot.

Don’t Draw a band metric as a floor

Sleep today draws taller for a 10h night than an 8h one. Height must mean “further from the goal”, not “more”.

C3

Reference rule

One dashed hairline across the plot at the goal value, labeled once at the right edge. It converts every bar from an absolute quantity into a comparison, and it lets the fill color become redundant reinforcement rather than the only channel carrying the goal.

Anatomy

.x-rule · .x-ruleK

1px dashed var(--t4), absolutely positioned by bottom: (goal / scaleMax) × 100%. The label sits above the right end on a --surface-raised chip so it never sits on a bar. The plot grows 46px → 64px; below that the rule and the tallest bar crowd each other.

Why a line, not a darker bar

rationale

Bar length is judged against a common baseline, which is the most accurate visual comparison available; a second aligned baseline at the goal makes “how far short” readable directly, with no legend. A fill-color step asks the reader to recall what the step means and cannot express distance at all. The rule also lets round goal numbers act as the sub-target readers naturally anchor to.

C4

Target band

The band is the reference rule for two-sided goals: a tinted zone between the low and high bound, dashed on both edges. It is the piece the app is missing entirely, and sleep — the tracker the product is named after — is the metric that needs it most.

Sleep, 7–9h band on a 10h scale

.x-band · .x-bandK

Fill is color-mix(in srgb, var(--ok) 20%, transparent) so it reads as a zone rather than a series. Bars inside are --a500; bars outside take the breach scale in either direction — a 9h30 night is flagged the same way a 6h10 night is, which is the entire point. The zone survives grayscale, so the band is doing the work and the hue is confirming it.

C5

Missing data and overshoot

Two ways the current charts quietly lie: an unlogged day is drawn as a very bad day, and a very good day is drawn as an ordinary one. Both are cheap to fix and both change what the user concludes.

Missing mark

.x-miss · .x-axMiss

No fill. A full-height dotted gutter and a hollow ring on the baseline, with the day letter dropped to --t5. Reads as “nothing here”, not as “nearly nothing”. Studies of missing data in charts find that substituting a default value stops readers noticing the gap at all, and that leaving the space empty with an explanation produces the highest decision confidence — hence the ring plus the footer denominator, not just a blank.

Overshoot cap

.x-over · .x-overTag

Scale to max(goal × 1.25, observed max) so a normal week never touches the ceiling. When a value still exceeds the scale, square off the bar’s top corners, inset a 3px --a200 cap, and print the real number above it. The cap says “this bar is truncated”; the number says by how much. Never clamp to 100 with no mark, which is what WeekStats.tsx does today.

C6

Breach scale

Warnings currently have no vocabulary at all: the semantic tokens --ok, --warn and --bad exist, are used by the goal-card status dot and the SUDS dial, and appear in zero charts. The chart system’s only alarm is a purple dot that is also the color of “today” and of “actionable”. Two levels, each with a non-color partner, fix both problems.

All six states in one plot

.x-hit · .x-warn · .x-bad · .x-today · .x-miss

The hatch is the accessibility load-bearer: WCAG 1.4.1 requires a second channel wherever color carries meaning, and roughly one in twelve men has a color-vision deficiency. Squint at this and the two breach bars still separate from the rest — that is the test.

Do Reserve --accent for now

Today, the live marker, the primary action. One meaning. Everything that was accent-because-it-is-bad becomes --warn or --bad.

Don’t Add a third breach level

Two is what a personal tracker can honestly distinguish. Bullet-graph practice caps qualitative ranges at three and recommends fewer; more states cost more reasoning than a glance affords.

Note Breach is not judgment

--bad describes the measurement’s distance from a user-set goal, never the person. Copy stays factual: “2h below your range”, not “bad night”.

C7

Pace marker

A goal card at 08:00 and the same card at 20:00 currently look identical: “112 / 160 g · 48 g to go”. But 112g is ahead in the morning and behind at night. The math for this already ships and is unit tested — paceMessage() in lib/nutrition/stats.ts. It just needs a mark on the bar.

Ahead of pace

.x-bar · .x-pace

Track grows 3px → 4px to seat the marker. A 1px --t2 tick with a small caret sits at expected / goal; the fill takes --a500 when it is past the tick and --warn when it is behind. This is the bullet-graph move: a featured measure, a comparative marker, and a qualitative range in one row.

Pace applies only to goals that accumulate within a day: protein, kcal, gym minutes, water. It is meaningless for sleep (measured once, at the end) and for cutoff goals — those cards keep a plain bar. The tracker’s goal shape decides.

C8

Cutoff track

The single biggest legibility win available. The caffeine dot row today packs intakes left-to-right in log order and encodes lateness as a color swap — so the dots carry no time information at all, and the one thing you want to see is the one thing you have to decode. Put the dots on a clock axis and lateness becomes a position past a line.

C9

Coverage foot

One row under every chart, mandatory, carrying the two things the manual already requires and no chart currently prints: a factual summary with its denominator, and how much of the period was actually logged. An average over 3 of 7 days is a different claim from an average over 7.

Full coverage

.x-foot · .x-cov
In range 5 of 7 nights7 / 7 logged

Denominator is logged days, never calendar days. The pill states which.

Thin coverage

.x-cov.is-thin
In range 2 of 3 logged nights3 / 7 logged

Below half the period the pill turns --warn and the summary names the denominator inline. Below two logged days the chart shows the bars but suppresses the summary entirely rather than averaging two points — insufficient evidence qualifies or suppresses the claim.

C10

Segmented hour bar

A sixth chart type, and the right one for sleep. A continuous bar makes you estimate “that looks like about seven hours”; a stack of one-hour blocks makes you count. It is the same argument the mood grid already wins — discrete units stay countable in a way a height never is — applied to the metric where the exact number is the whole point.

Sleep · one block per hour · goal 7h, band 7–9h

.x-hours · .x-hourCol

Ten blocks per column on a 0–10h scale, 2px gaps, empty blocks left as --rail so the scale stays visible on a short night. Hours below the goal are --a800; hours inside the band above it are --a500; hours past the band top are --warn. A day that finished short recolors all its blocks to the breach tone, because the verdict is on the night, not on the individual hour. The final part-hour renders at 45% opacity.

Read it without a legend: Thursday is six blocks and change, Sunday is five and a half, Monday runs past the band. The goal line is at a block boundary, which is what makes the count and the rule agree instead of competing.

Do Use it where the unit is the point

Sleep hours, gym sets, meals, cups. If a user would say the number out loud — “I got six hours” — the blocks should be countable.

Don’t Segment a continuous quantity

Protein grams keep the plain bar. Chopping 160g into blocks invents a unit the user does not think in and costs the resolution the rule needs.

C11

Column tooltip

The chart should answer the follow-up question in place. Today, seeing that Sunday was short means opening the ledger to find out when you went to bed. The tooltip carries the date, the value, the verdict against the goal, and what made the number up — so a week review never leaves the chart.

Anatomy

.x-tip · .x-tipD · .x-tipV · .x-tipS · .x-tipRow

Date eyebrow, value at 15px, one verdict line in the state color, then composition rows under a hairline. On --sheet with --sh-md, matching the timeline tooltip already in the app rather than inventing a second popover style.

The third row is the cross-domain one: the late coffee that probably shortened that night is right there, stated as an aligned observation and never as a cause.

Three ways in

.x-hit-area · :focus-visible · .is-open

A tooltip that only answers to a mouse fails half this app’s usage. Each column gets a transparent button covering the bar and its axis label — a real hit target, not a title attribute.

InputBehavior
HoverOpens on the column, 150ms fade with a 3px rise.
KeyboardEach column is one tab stop; :focus-visible opens the tooltip and draws an accent outline. Escape closes without moving focus.
TapToggles .is-open; tapping elsewhere or the same column closes. This is the mobile path, so it is the one that must not regress.
Screen readerThe button’s accessible name is the whole sentence — “Sunday 24 July, 5 hours 35 minutes, 1 hour 25 below your range” — so the tooltip is never the only route to the value.
Reduced motionOpens with no transition. The tooltip still appears; only the fade and rise are dropped.

Edge columns clamp horizontally so the popover never leaves the card. The tooltip is supplementary by contract: everything in it is also reachable from the chart’s data table.

C12

Week matrix

“How is my week going?” is a question no current screen answers. Four separate charts each answer one metric, and nothing shows them together. The matrix is one row per tracker, one column per day, one glyph per cell — the whole week across every domain, in about a second.

Five trackers · seven days

.x-matrix · .x-cell

Each cell is a 26px chip carrying a shape, not just a color: check for in goal, caret up for over, caret down for under, hollow for not logged, split fill for in progress. The glyph is what makes it readable in grayscale and to a colorblind reader — the fill is reinforcement. Today is a tinted column rail, never a different chip color, so “which day is now” and “how did that day go” stay on separate channels.

That column-wise juxtaposition is the “clear image of how my week is going” — and it stays an observation, with no arrow drawn between the two rows.

Do Ship it as a real table

<table> with scope on both header axes. The matrix is simultaneously the glanceable view and the structured data view C15 asks for — one component satisfies both.

Don’t Score the week

No percentage, no grade, no streak. The header states a count over a denominator and stops there.

Note This is the mobile answer

Mobile has no week view at all today. The matrix is compact enough for a 28rem column, which makes it the natural first week surface on the phone.

Mood keeps its grid

.mgrid — unchanged

Worth saying explicitly: the stacked count grid is the one chart in the app that is already right. Mood is a 1–5 ordinal, the grid keeps the units countable, and it has no goal — so it takes no rule, no band, and no breach color. It gains only the missing-day treatment (an empty column reads as absent already) and a coverage foot. Do not add a target to a metric the user has not set one for.

C14

Color semantics after the change

No new tokens. The change is that the semantic trio finally reaches the charts, and the accent ramp goes back to meaning one thing.

TokenMeant, in chartsNow means
--accentToday, and a late caffeine intakeNow / today only — as an underline below the baseline, never as a fill.
--a500Hit the targetUnchanged: satisfies the goal shape.
--a800Ordinary barUnchanged: logged, no goal verdict.
--a900Low and missingRetired from charts. Low is --warn; missing has no fill.
--warnUnused in chartsSoft breach, plus the cutoff rule and its zone.
--badUnused in chartsHard breach.
--okUnused in chartsThe target band tint and its dashed edges, at low alpha.
--t4Axis ticksAlso the reference rule.
--t5Axis ticks, disabled metaAlso the missing-day gutter, ring and letter.

The one judgment call: --warn and --bad arrive in charts carrying connotations the goal-language rules push back on. They are acceptable because they describe the measurement’s relation to a goal the user set, and because every one of them is paired with factual copy and a denominator. If a tracker has no goal, it gets no breach color — that is the guard.

C15

The structured data view

The manual has required an equivalent way to read every chart since it was written; no chart has one. The cheapest conformant answer that fits this app: each chart renders a real table as its accessible sibling, visually hidden, plus a visible toggle on desktop.

Shape

proposed

The plot becomes role="img" with an aria-label carrying the summary sentence — “Sleep, last 7 days. In range 3 of 6 logged nights. Friday not logged.” — and the decorative bars stay hidden. The table below is the real record and is what a screen reader, a keyboard user, and anyone who wants the number reads. Bars stay non-interactive: they are not buttons today, and making seven <i> elements focusable to expose values a table already lists would add a tab stop per day for nothing.
